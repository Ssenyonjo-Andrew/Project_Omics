About Viral XAI

Viral XAI is a research prototype designed to evaluate the reliability of deep learning models in viral genomic surveillance. It goes beyond simple classification by examining how models make their decisions.

The Three Pillars of XAI

  • Stability: Spearman correlation between models' attribution rankings (Cross-Model Agreement).
  • Fidelity: Drop in predicted probability when top windows are masked.
  • Plausibility: Observed vs Expected overlap of AI attention with annotated genes.

Supported Viruses

Dengue, Chikungunya, Norovirus, Measles, Mpox, and Adenovirus C.

How to read this dashboard

High accuracy does not mean the model learned biology. This dashboard audits the AI's reasoning across three pillars:

1. Stability

Do the CNN, BiLSTM, and Transformer agree on which genomic regions matter? Measured via Spearman correlation. A score near 1.0 means they agree. A score near 0.0 means they are looking at completely different regions.

2. Fidelity

If we delete the top regions the AI says are important, does the prediction break? A large drop in confidence means the model is actually relying on those regions. A small drop means it found redundant signals elsewhere.

3. Biological Plausibility

Does the AI focus on annotated biological genes (green blocks) or intergenic noise? Measured via an Observed/Expected (O/E) ratio. An O/E score above 1.0 means the AI looks at real genes more often than random chance.
